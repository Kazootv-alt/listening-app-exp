# 駅で道をたずねる
Ken arrived at a busy train station in London at 8:15 a.m. He had a map on his phone, but the battery was almost empty.
He needed to get to a small museum near the river, and he was not sure which line to take.
So he decided to do something he usually avoided. He decided to ask a stranger for help.
He walked up to a woman who was reading the departure board and said, "Excuse me, could you help me for a moment?"
She smiled and said, "Of course. Where are you going?"
Ken showed her the name of the museum. "I think I need the blue line, but I'm not sure."
"You're close," she said. "Take the blue line two stops, then change to the green line. Get off at Riverside. It's about a 5 minute walk from there."
Ken repeated the directions slowly to make sure he understood. "Two stops on the blue line, change to the green line, and get off at Riverside."
"Exactly," she said. "And if you get lost, just ask someone at the station. People here are usually happy to help."
On the train, Ken thought about the conversation. It had taken less than one minute, but he had used more English than he had all week.
He noticed that the most useful phrases were very simple. Excuse me. Could you help me? Where are you going? I'm not sure.
When he reached Riverside, the walk to the museum took 4.5 minutes, almost exactly what the woman had said.
That evening, Ken wrote the phrases in his notebook. Next time, he decided, he would not wait until his battery died to start a conversation.
